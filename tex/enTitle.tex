\latinkeywords{Magnetically levitated planar motor, six-degree-of-freedom control, current allocation, magnetic surrogate model, vision feedback, physical prototype}
\en-abstract{
Contactless six-degree-of-freedom motion in a magnetically levitated planar motor (MLPM) requires continuous stabilization and coordination of magnetic modeling, sensing, control, and electrical actuation. This thesis developed and evaluated an integrated framework from a reference simulation to a physical prototype. The reference system combined MATLAB/Simulink, ROS~2, and Gazebo with fuzzy--PID control, current allocation under actuator constraints, and trajectory generation. Six-coordinate step responses, pose-dependent actuation conditioning, and Manhattan-trajectory tracking were evaluated; the two horizontal step responses settled in approximately $1.12\,\mathrm{s}$. A physical prototype was then built with a two-dimensional Halbach mover and eight active coils. A geometry-specific magnetic model was computed using Magpylib, and a surrogate was fitted for online current allocation. On held-out numerical test data, its six output components achieved $R^2$ values of $0.9546$--$0.9742$ and normalized root-mean-square errors of $16.05\%$--$21.30\%$ relative to the numerical model. Camera-based AprilTag pose estimation was integrated with the controller and electrical drivers. Mechanically constrained planar, height, and orientation experiments preceded a physical run in which all six coordinates were controlled simultaneously for approximately $38.4\,\mathrm{s}$. Full-interval, vision-referenced root-mean-square errors were $0.1829\,\mathrm{mm}$, $0.1726\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $0.9916\,\mathrm{mm}$ for $x$, $y$, and $z$, and $0.2899^\circ$, $0.2031^\circ$, and $0.1866^\circ$ for roll, pitch, and yaw, respectively. The experiment demonstrates simultaneous six-coordinate feedback control within a restricted operating region and establishes an experimental basis for coordinated electromagnetic, sensing, and actuation design.
}
